BrowsePocket is an Android web browser by SouMoster. This is the privacy policy for the app. The website has its own policy.

## What stays on the phone

Your tabs, bookmarks, history, reading list, pages saved for offline reading, the list of downloads, site settings and preferences are all stored on your device. SouMoster does not offer an account or sync, and the app does not show ads, use analytics or send crash reports. It also opts out of the usage statistics that Android System WebView can collect. SouMoster never receives your data.

## The web engine

BrowsePocket shows web pages with [Android System WebView](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.webview), which is provided by Google and kept up to date through Google Play.

Safe Browsing is on by default. It checks the pages you open against Google's lists of phishing and malware sites, and to do that WebView may send Google information about the addresses you visit. Google handles this under the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). You can turn Safe Browsing off in Settings → Privacy & security.

## Websites you visit

The sites you open receive what any browser sends them, such as your IP address and the cookies they have set, and they handle it under their own privacy policies. BrowsePocket blocks third-party cookies, and it asks sites not to sell or share your data by sending Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track. You can change each of these in Settings.

## Searching

What you search for goes to the search engine you chose, DuckDuckGo unless you pick another. Search suggestions are off until you turn them on. When they're on, what you type in the address bar goes to that search engine as you type. Suggestions are never fetched in private tabs.

## Content blocking

Ads, trackers and cookie banners are blocked using EasyList and EasyPrivacy, which come with the app. Once a week, on Wi-Fi, BrowsePocket downloads the latest lists from easylist.to. The request carries nothing about you, though like any web request it shows the server your device's IP address. Cookie banners are hidden and never clicked, so no consent is given for you.

## Private tabs

Private tabs use their own cookies, site storage and cache, separate from your normal tabs. Their pages are not added to history, and nothing about them is written to the app's storage. Everything they held is deleted when the last private tab closes, or the next time the app starts if it was closed unexpectedly. Screenshots and the recent-apps preview are blocked while a private tab is on screen.

## Camera, microphone and location

A site can use your camera, microphone or location only if it asks and you allow it, and Android asks for its own permission the first time too. Your choice is kept for that site, and you can change it from the site information sheet or in Settings → Site settings. Photos you take or files you choose for a site go to that site.

## App lock

App lock and the private-tabs lock use your device's own lock screen or a PIN for BrowsePocket. Your fingerprint or face is checked by Android, and the app never sees it. A PIN is stored only as a salted hash.

## Downloads

Files you download are saved to Downloads/BrowsePocket on your phone. If ReadPocket is installed, you can choose to open a downloaded PDF there.

## AI features

AI features are optional and stay off until you paste your own OpenAI API key in Settings. The key is encrypted on the phone, is sent only to OpenAI, and is left out of Android backups and device-to-device transfer. When you tap Summarise page or Ask about this page, the text of that page and its title go, along with your question. Very long pages are cut to their first part. When you tap Explain on selected text, that text and the page's title go. AI is never offered in private tabs, and nothing is sent automatically or in the background. It goes directly from your phone to OpenAI over an encrypted connection, and OpenAI handles it under its own terms and privacy policy. Usage is billed to your OpenAI account. SouMoster never receives your key or the text. To stop, remove the key in Settings.

## Sharing and other apps

Apart from the sites you visit and the AI features above, your data leaves BrowsePocket only when you send it: sharing a link, sending a page or a reply to NotesPocket, opening a PDF in ReadPocket, or opening a link in another app, which BrowsePocket asks you about first. That app gets only what you sent, and handles it under its own privacy policy.

## Backups and deleting your data

Nothing from BrowsePocket goes into Android backups or device-to-device transfer. The backup in Settings is a file you choose where to keep. It holds your bookmarks, reading list, pinned sites, site settings, search engines and preferences, plus your history and offline pages if you include them. Cookies, open tabs and lock settings are never in it. If you set a password, the file is encrypted with AES-256, and the password is never stored. You can also export your bookmarks as an HTML file.

To delete your data, use Settings → Privacy & security to clear history, cookies, cache or tabs, or have them cleared each time the app closes. To remove everything, use Android Settings → Apps → BrowsePocket → Storage → Clear storage. Downloads stay in Downloads/BrowsePocket until you delete them. SouMoster holds no BrowsePocket data, so there is nothing to request from us.
